# Subramanian

**Medal:** George Cross (posthumous), first Indian recipient — gazetted 30 June 1944. Also Indian Distinguished Service Medal.

## Script

Subedar Subramanian pushed a man away from a live mine. Then he took the blast himself.

He was born in nineteen twelve, in the small village of Ottivakkam, near Kanchipuram, in Tamil Nadu. He served with the Madras Sappers and Miners, engineers trained to build bridges and clear the ground ahead of an advancing army.

By February nineteen forty-four, the Allies were stuck below Monte Cassino, trying to force a way through the Gustav Line towards Rome. Before any attack could go in, the approach roads and fields had to be cleared of mines. It was slow, dangerous work, done on hands and knees. The battle for Monte Cassino would last four months, from January to May nineteen forty-four, and become one of the costliest battles of the Italian campaign. The abbey itself did not fall until the eighteenth of May.

On the twenty-fourth of February, near Mignano, Subramanian was leading his men through one such minefield, marking the cleared path behind him as he went.

Then he realised the man behind him had triggered a mine. It would explode in seconds.

There was no time to warn him properly. There was only time to act.

Subramanian pushed his comrade clear, and took the blast himself.

He died shortly afterwards.

Work like this rarely makes the news. A single missed mine could stop an entire attack, so the sappers had to be right every time. The roads his unit cleared were part of the groundwork for the offensive that, months later, broke through at Cassino and opened the way to Rome. Men like Subramanian made that possible by making the ground safe to walk on.

For saving the life of the man behind him, Subramanian became the first Indian to be awarded the George Cross.

His name is on a memorial wall in London, and there is a memorial to him in the village where he was born.

Every sapper who clears a path does so at risk to their own life, so that the men who follow can walk safely. Subramanian did that to the very end, and his courage is still part of how the Cassino campaign is remembered.

Subramanian. Remember him.
