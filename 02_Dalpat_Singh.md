# Dalpat Singh

**Medal:** Military Cross (Abu Tulul, 14 July 1918). Killed in action leading the charge at Haifa, 23 September 1918 — commemorated as "Hero of Haifa"; India marks Haifa Day on 23 September.

## Script

Hoofbeats. Smoke. A machine gun dug into the hill ahead.

Thakur Dalpat Singh rides straight at it, just him and his trumpeter beside him.

This isn't the first time.

Dalpat Singh was born in eighteen ninety-two, in Jodhpur State, in what's now Rajasthan. He served with the Jodhpur Lancers, cavalry raised not by the British government directly, but by an Indian princely state that chose to send its own soldiers to fight a war thousands of miles from home.

At Abu Tulul, in July nineteen eighteen, he'd charged a machine-gun post with just his trumpeter beside him, scattering its crew and capturing the gun, along with its commandant. Bravery that earned him the Military Cross.

Two months later, he's doing it again.

It's the twenty-third of September, nineteen eighteen. The place is Haifa, a port city on the Mediterranean coast, in what was then Ottoman Palestine.

This charge isn't a small skirmish. It's the climax of one of the largest Allied offensives of the First World War, the Battle of Megiddo, under General Allenby. Over the previous four days, the Ottoman Seventh and Eighth Armies had been broken apart, their retreat turning into a rout. Securing Haifa's port would help the Allies control the coastline, and cut off what was left of the Ottoman army's ability to resupply.

Dalpat Singh leads his Jodhpur Lancers straight at the guns defending the city.

The guns open up. He keeps riding.

He reaches the position. The defences break. By the end of the day, Haifa has fallen.

But Dalpat Singh doesn't ride away this time. Bullets have found him. He dies within hours, thousands of miles from the home he grew up in.

His bravery had already been proven once, at Abu Tulul.

At Haifa, it became his sacrifice.

Within six weeks of that charge, the Ottoman Empire's war in the Middle East was effectively over, and by the end of October, it had signed an armistice. Haifa's fall was one piece of that collapse, not the whole of it, but it was Dalpat Singh who led the charge that took the city.

Every year, on the twenty-third of September, India marks Haifa Day, for a charge, and a soldier, most people have never heard of.

Say his name. Dalpat Singh.

## Open question
No source confirms a separate gallantry award specifically for the Haifa action — the MC was for Abu Tulul. Script treats these as two distinct events (bravery vs. sacrifice). Sources also differ on exactly which cavalry units fought at Haifa itself (Jodhpur/Mysore Lancers vs. Hyderabad Lancers detached guarding prisoners) — script keeps to the Jodhpur Lancers, consistent with the CSV and Wikipedia.
